Starting a vending machine business in Texas requires a general business license from the Texas Comptroller's coin-operated machine program ($200 to $500 a year depending on how many machines you own), a $60-per-year occupation tax decal for every individual machine, and a state sales tax permit, since Texas taxes most vending sales as retail transactions. If any machine dispenses perishable or prepared food, add a local or state health department food permit on top of those requirements. Texas is one of a handful of states that regulates vending machines specifically as "coin-operated machines" through its Comptroller's office, which is a different structure than most states use.

This licensing sequence is Texas-specific. If you're comparing requirements across states generally, see our broader [vending machine business license](/vending-machine-business-license/) guide.

## What license does the Texas Comptroller actually require?

A general business license, priced on a sliding scale by machine count, is required to own, buy, sell, lease, or exhibit coin-operated machines anywhere in Texas, and every individual machine needs its own $60 annual occupation tax decal visibly attached to it.

| Texas Comptroller fee (filed by Nov. 30) | Amount |
|---|---|
| General business license, 50 or fewer machines | $200/year |
| General business license, 51 to 200 machines | $400/year |
| General business license, more than 200 machines | $500/year |
| Occupation tax decal | $60/year per machine |
| Registration certificate (machines only in your own place of business) | $150/year |

Source: [Texas Comptroller, Coin-Operated Machines Taxes and Fees](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/coin-op/). Fees increase on a quarterly schedule if you file after the Nov. 30 deadline, so a business license filed after April 1 costs double the on-time rate. All renewals are due annually on Nov. 30, and license applicants must be at least 18 years old.

## Do you need a different license if you're a small, single-location operator?

Possibly a registration certificate instead of the full general business license, if you only own or exhibit machines in your own place of business, have no machines in another person's location, and have no other financial interest in the coin-operated machine industry.

That's a narrower category than most vending entrepreneurs fit, since the entire point of a vending route is placing machines in other people's locations (offices, gyms, laundromats). Most operators starting an actual vending route, rather than putting one machine in their own retail storefront, need the general business license, not the cheaper registration certificate. Check the exact definition against your plan before assuming the lower-cost path applies to you.

## Do you need a separate sales tax permit on top of the coin-operated machine license?

Yes. The coin-operated machine license and occupation tax are separate from Texas sales tax obligations, and vending sales are generally taxable retail transactions requiring their own sales tax permit from the Comptroller, applied for separately and at no cost.

Texas sales tax permits are free to apply for through the Comptroller's online portal, distinct from the coin-operated machine fees above, which do cost money. Both are administered by the same agency (the Texas Comptroller of Public Accounts), which makes it easy to apply for both in the same session, but they're legally separate requirements and both are mandatory for a standard vending operation.

## What about a health permit for food or coffee vending machines in Texas?

If a machine dispenses perishable or potentially hazardous food, rather than shelf-stable packaged snacks, Texas generally requires a food establishment permit issued by your local city or county health department, or by the Texas Department of State Health Services in areas without a local health authority.

This applies most directly to fresh food, dairy-based, or temperature-controlled vending, not standard snack, soda, or shelf-stable coffee machines. An initial inspection is typically required before the permit is issued. Because permitting for retail food establishments in Texas is largely handled at the local level, requirements and fees vary by city and county, so confirm directly with your local health department before assuming a state-level number applies to your specific machine and location. See the [Texas DSHS retail food establishment permitting page](https://www.dshs.texas.gov/retail-food-establishments/permitting-information-retail-food-establishments) for the state-level framework and to find your local jurisdiction's contact.

## Snippet-ready: Texas vending machine business setup checklist

1. Register an LLC or sole proprietorship with the Texas Secretary of State ($40 to $500 in filing fees, LLC not legally required but common for liability protection).
2. Apply for a Texas Comptroller general business coin-operated machine license ($200 to $500/year, sliding scale by machine count).
3. Purchase the $60 annual occupation tax decal for each individual machine and attach it visibly.
4. Apply for a free Texas sales tax permit through the Comptroller's portal.
5. If dispensing perishable food, apply for a local or DSHS food establishment permit before placing the machine.
6. File all coin-operated machine renewals by Nov. 30 each year to avoid the escalating late fee schedule.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much does it cost to license a vending machine business in Texas?**
A general business license from the Texas Comptroller costs $200 to $500 a year depending on machine count, plus a $60 annual occupation tax decal per individual machine. A separate Texas sales tax permit is free to apply for.

**Does Texas require a special vending machine tax?**
Yes. Texas taxes coin-operated machines, including vending machines, through the Comptroller's coin-operated machine program, which requires a general business license and a $60 per-machine annual occupation tax, separate from standard sales tax.

**Do I need a food permit to run a snack vending machine in Texas?**
Generally no for shelf-stable, packaged snacks and drinks. A food establishment permit typically applies only to machines dispensing perishable or potentially hazardous food, and is issued by your local city or county health department in most of Texas.

**When are Texas coin-operated machine license renewals due?**
All coin-operated machine licenses and occupation tax decals renew annually on Nov. 30. Filing after that date triggers an escalating late fee schedule, with fees roughly doubling if filed after April 1 of the following year.

**Can I operate a vending machine business in Texas without an LLC?**
Yes, an LLC isn't legally required to operate, but most operators form one for personal liability protection since machines are placed in public and semi-public locations. LLC filing through the Texas Secretary of State costs $300 for a standard filing.
